# Nextdoor and LinkedIn feed mechanics a local-services marketplace could adopt (research notes, current to 2026-11-14)

Sourcing convention: "(via search excerpt)" means the claim came from a search-engine summary of that page, not from a full fetch of the page. Treat those claims as less certain than the rest. Company figures (Nextdoor transparency reports, LinkedIn engineering results) are self-reported unless noted. Older behavior is labeled with its year.

## 1. Nextdoor: post types, feed scope and ranking, reply threads, reactions, Faves, business recommendations, address verification, moderation, anti-profiling prompts, digests, and how posts surface local service providers

### Takeaway
Nextdoor's engine is **verified locality plus recommendations**. Local recommendations are about 30% of all content. Neighbor tags turn into business preview cards that show a recommendation count. "Faves" are vouches that only count from verified neighbors who hired the business, and they feed search rank and yearly per-neighborhood awards. The safety layer was built after public failures: pre-post friction prompts (Kindness Reminder, the anti-racism notification, the 2016 structured crime form), community moderation by consensus with no power over accounts, and staff-only handling of racism and misinformation reports. Since 2025–2026 the feed has been ML-ranked. It pushes self-promotion down, adds local news and household-targeted alerts, uses AI "Ask" answers built from past conversations, and groups replies with author-pinned comments.

"Nearly 30% of all content on Nextdoor is organic local recommendations" — [2026 Fave Awards release, Sept 10, 2026](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoor-opens-voting-for-the-2026-fave-awards).

- **#ThankANeighbor** (Nov 2023) is a dedicated composer option. The poster picks one of five themes ("Say thanks," "Act of kindness," "Helped me," "Generous," "Friendly"), enters "the name of the person or group they would like to acknowledge," and writes what prompted the thanks. It also has a standalone entry point (nextdoor.com/thanks). One showcased post got "nearly 5,000 reactions." — [Nextdoor blog, Nov 3, 2023](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2023/11/03/nextdoor-thank-a-neighbor)
- **Older (2012):** the "Nearby Neighborhoods" audience was pitched for getting local service recommendations, listing items for sale to a wider group, and alerting neighbors about nearby criminal activity. — [Nextdoor blog, Sept 13, 2012](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2012/09/13/communicate-with-even-more-neighbors/) (via search excerpt)
- **Crime & Safety** had its own guided posting flow from 2016 (see the anti-profiling subsection). — [CBS News, Sept 8, 2016](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nextdoor-social-network-neighborhoods-racial-profiling-decline-after-changes)
- **News** (July 2025): stories from vetted local publishers appear in the feed, "tailored to the neighbor's location." It launched with more than 3,500 publications across the US, UK and Canada, covers 77% of US cities on Nextdoor, and delivers more than 50,000 local stories a week. — [Meet the New Nextdoor](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/meet-the-new-nextdoor). The app shows headlines and photos and links out to the publishers' sites. — [TechCrunch, July 15, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/15/nextdoor-redesigns-app-with-ai-recommendations-local-news-and-real-time-emergency-alerts) (via search excerpt)

Data comes from Samdesk, PowerOutage.US and The Weather Company. — [Meet the New Nextdoor](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/meet-the-new-nextdoor). Risk is color-coded green, yellow or red, and alerts can target specific households, for example a power-outage alert sent only to affected homes. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/15/nextdoor-redesigns-app-with-ai-recommendations-local-news-and-real-time-emergency-alerts) (via search excerpt)
- **Journalist profiles** (pilot, April 2026) let neighbors follow and message local reporters. — [Nextdoor, "What's New on Nextdoor: Product Updates," Apr 22, 2026](https://blog.nextdoor.com/whats-new-on-nextdoor-product-updates-1)

#### Audience and feed scope
- The poster picks one of three audiences: **"Anyone"** (posts are "shareable with anyone on or off Nextdoor"), **"Nearby neighborhoods"** ("the neighborhoods close to you"), or **"Your neighborhood"** ("your neighborhood only"). "You are always in control of your audience when you post." Even so, Nextdoor "may distribute" relevant posts to "additional neighborhoods beyond the poster's Nearby Neighborhoods." No radius or relevance rule is published. — [Nextdoor blog, Oct 20, 2020](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2020/10/20/new-features-help-you-stay-connected-to-more-of-the-neighborhoods-you-care-about)
- **Older (2012):** "you decide when and if you want to post to nearby neighborhoods," which was opt-in widening. — [Nextdoor blog, 2012](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2012/09/13/communicate-with-even-more-neighbors/) (via search excerpt)
- A third-party summary of Nextdoor's privacy policy lists "specific Groups" as another audience. — [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/nextdoor/nextdoor-privacy-policy/user-controls-post-audience-visibility/). Groups exist, and Group Admins moderate reported group content. — [Nextdoor, "How to moderate on Nextdoor"](https://go.us.nextdoor.com/learn-about-moderation) (via search excerpt)

#### Feed ranking and the 2025–2026 redesign
- Nextdoor called the July 15, 2025 relaunch "the most consequential redesign of its core product to date," built around News, Alerts and an AI recommendations feature. The release calls that feature "Ask" in one place and "Faves" in another. — [Meet the New Nextdoor](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/meet-the-new-nextdoor). Coverage describes "a cleaner feed, a news carousel and AI-generated prompts to spark discussion." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/15/nextdoor-redesigns-app-with-ai-recommendations-local-news-and-real-time-emergency-alerts); [Axios, July 15, 2025](https://www.axios.com/2025/07/15/nextdoor-app-ai-reboot) (via search excerpts)
- **"Ask" (AI)** summarizes "14 years of neighborhood conversations" about local favorites to answer questions such as "What is the best place to hike with kids?" It launched first in select US markets. — [Meet the New Nextdoor](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/meet-the-new-nextdoor)
- **April 2026 ranking changes:** "We continue to use machine learning to better understand which posts and conversations are most likely to matter to you." ML-driven suggestions now reach neighbors who haven't visited recently, and in early tests those neighbors stayed longer and engaged more deeply. Self-promotional and low-quality posts appear less often, "especially at the top," and new neighbors see more local conversations and fewer promotions. — [Nextdoor product updates, Apr 22, 2026](https://blog.nextdoor.com/whats-new-on-nextdoor-product-updates-1)
- Year-end "Rewind" picks top posts by "the number of likes, comments, shares and reactions received." — [Nextdoor Rewind 2024 release](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoor-rewind-returns-nextdoor) (via search excerpt)

#### Comments, replies and reactions
- **April 2026:** "Replies now stay neatly grouped together, so it's easier to follow who said what and when." Post authors can **pin a comment** to the top of a thread, such as a helpful answer or an important update. Links in comments show richer previews. Short celebratory animations play after posting. — [Nextdoor product updates, Apr 22, 2026](https://blog.nextdoor.com/whats-new-on-nextdoor-product-updates-1)
- **Reactions:** "We're experimenting with more expressive default reactions, like a heart instead of a thumbs-up." — [same](https://blog.nextdoor.com/whats-new-on-nextdoor-product-updates-1)

#### How posts surface local service providers: tags, preview cards, Business Pages, Faves
- **2019:** neighbors "can now tag businesses that they recommend," and "All recommendations will be associated with that business's Nextdoor page." Tagged posts appear on the business's page. — [Nextdoor AU blog, July 2019](https://aublog.nextdoor.com/2019/07/14/introducing-local-business-pages-and-recommendations) (via search excerpt)
- **2021:** a tag in a post or comment renders a **preview card** with the business's name, business information, profile photo and **recommendation count**, all pulled from its Business Page. The tagged business is notified. Nextdoor claimed "Business Page views have gone up 18%" after launch. — [Nextdoor Business, June 2021](https://business.nextdoor.com/local/resources/ask-a-neighbor-how-your-small-business-can-take-advantage-of-a-new-nextdoor-feature)
- **Faves (2026):** a Fave is Nextdoor's signal "that a neighbor vouches for a business." Per the release it comes from a verified neighbor who has actually hired the business, and the count is visible on the Business Page. "The more Faves a business has, the higher it appears in the annual Fave Awards voting flow, search." Voting ran Sept 10–30, 2026 in the US, Canada and UK. Winners get a window sticker, a trophy badge on their Business Page, and featured placement in their neighborhood's "Local Faves" discovery hub, so "every neighborhood will come away with a curated list of the businesses its neighbors rely on." Businesses get a unique Fave link to share by text, email, print or social. Nextdoor says past winners average 32× more Business Page views, 26× more recommendations and 35% more ad clicks than non-winners. Nearly 6.6M businesses have claimed a free Business Page. — [2026 Fave Awards release](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoor-opens-voting-for-the-2026-fave-awards)
- A 2026 third-party app review claims Nextdoor now charges some service businesses to comment on neighbors' requests. This is unverified and not official policy. — [Unstar, 2026](https://unstar.app/blog/nextdoor-business-paywall-cant-comment-suspended-2026)

- **Real-name policy:** "every neighbor signs in using their real name." — [Wikipedia: Nextdoor](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nextdoor) (via search excerpt). Nextdoor's privacy policy lets members choose "the degree to which personal information, such as your full name, is shared in your personal profile and alongside your posts." — [ConductAtlas summary of Nextdoor privacy policy](https://conductatlas.com/platform/nextdoor/nextdoor-privacy-policy/user-controls-degree-of-profile-information-sharing/)

#### Moderation: friction prompts, community moderators, staff
- **Kindness Reminder** (launched Sept 18, 2019, US-wide): when a member replies with "a potentially offensive or hurtful comment," a prompt appears *before* publishing. It offers a chance to read the Community Guidelines, edit the reply, or not post it. It "uses data gathered from comments previously flagged by neighbors" with machine learning. Early tests: "1 in 5 people who saw Kindness Reminder hit 'edit' on their comment, resulting in 20% fewer negative comments." It was built with an advisory panel that included bias researcher Dr. Jennifer Eberhardt. — [Nextdoor press release, Sept 2019](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoor-announces-new-feature-to-promote-kindness-in-neighborhoods)
- **2023 (reported Feb 2024):** generative AI now *suggests a rewrite* of flagged comments. It was reviewed under Nextdoor's Generative AI Ethics Principles and audited by its Neighborhood Vitality Advisory Board. 36% of neighbors who saw either reminder edited or withheld their content, 26% of those shown the AI rewrite adopted it, and exposure cut creation of guideline-violating content by 15%. "Kindness Tips" now remind neighbors who had content removed before about best practices, and appeals were expanded. Reports of harmful content were 0.29% of all content. "A small group of neighbors, less than half a percent, produce almost 60% of removed content, mainly in comments." Nearly 200,000 volunteer moderators reviewed 90% of reported content, and the median removal time was 5.3 hours. — [Nextdoor 2023 Transparency Report release, Feb 13, 2024](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoors-transparency-report-highlights-impact-of-ai-led-innovation-maintaining-very-low-levels-of-harmful-content-reported-on-the-platform)
- **2022:** 210,900 volunteer moderators reviewed 92% of reported content and removed 57% of it, in a median 5.1 hours. Authors were notified of removals, told which guideline applied, and could appeal. — [Nextdoor, "Our commitment to transparency" (updated Feb 23, 2023)](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2022/02/23/our-commitment-to-transparency) (via search excerpt). For 2021, edit rates were reported at 34.4% (Anti-Racism Notification) and 34.6% (Kindness Reminder). — [StockTitan summary of the 2021 report](https://stocktitan.net/news/KIND/nextdoor-publishes-first-annual-2021-transparency-kshg4ow6i11c.html) (third-party)
- **Roles (Feb 2021):** staff "Neighborhood Operations" are "trained specialists," and reports of **misinformation and racism go only to staff** with "special training." Volunteer **Neighborhood Leads and Community Reviewers** "have to apply" and then vote on whether reported posts or comments break the guidelines in their own neighborhood. They "have no insight into neighbor reports" and lack "any ability to take action on a neighbor's account." Reported accounts are always reviewed by staff. — [Nextdoor blog, "How moderation works on Nextdoor," Feb 10, 2021](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2021/02/10/how-moderation-works-on-nextdoor)
- **Current moderation page:** any neighbor can report a post or comment. Reported items go into the queue of each moderator eligible to vote on them, and "a single moderator is not responsible for making the decision" for a community. "When a consensus is reached by local moderators, our moderation platform takes action." Nextdoor removes privileges after "a pattern of voting against the guidelines." — [Nextdoor, "How to moderate on Nextdoor"](https://go.us.nextdoor.com/learn-about-moderation) (via search excerpt). A reviewer's account lists the vote options as Keep, Maybe remove, Remove, or skip, and says the author isn't notified of individual votes. — [Ask Dave Taylor](https://www.askdavetaylor.com/how-nextdoor-content-moderation-works-from-a-review-team-member) (third-party, via search excerpt)
- **Anti-racism notification** (Apr 19, 2021): it triggers on phrases such as "All Lives Matter" or "Blue Lives Matter" and asks the author to reconsider, without blocking the post. It rolled out on web, iOS and Android. In the same coverage Nextdoor said Kindness Reminder had cut "incivil content" by 30%, and CEO Sarah Friar said the company was working to diversify its volunteer moderators. — [Engadget, Apr 19, 2021](https://www.engadget.com/nextdoor-anti-racism-notification-180204693.html)

#### Anti-racial-profiling design (2016)
- The Crime & Safety flow told posters to "stop and think before you post," asking in effect whether they would share the post if the person were of a different race. "If you choose to go ahead and invoke race as a descriptor, we have a higher bar": the poster has to give "a fuller description of the person," including "hair color, shoes, age and build." "Descriptions that are too short are also not accepted," and "an algorithm also flags racially-charged items." Result: "a 75 percent drop in posts containing racial profiling in certain test markets." Profiling had been "less than one percent of the posts." Some users complained it was "blocking my right to post," and Tolia replied, "This isn't about censorship." — [CBS News, Sept 8, 2016](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nextdoor-social-network-neighborhoods-racial-profiling-decline-after-changes)
- Earlier steps were a racial-profiling report flag on posts, updated member guidelines, and a mandatory warning screen before posting in Crime and Safety. The forms went live in all 110,000 neighborhoods. The CEO said the team manually examined "thousands and thousands of posts" and acknowledged a "business cost," because the friction makes posting less likely. — [BuzzFeed News, 2016](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/carolineodonovan/nextdoor-rolls-out-product-fix-it-hopes-will-stem-racial-pro); [GeekWire Q&A, 2016](https://www.geekwire.com/2016/qa-nextdoor-ceo-explains-social-network-cracking-racial-profiling/); [Nextdoor blog, Aug 24, 2016 (now 404)](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2016/08/24/reducing-racial-profiling-on-nextdoor) (all via search excerpts)

#### Notifications and digests
- A 2026 third-party guide says Nextdoor sends **daily and weekly digests**, **"trending in your neighborhood"** posts, and **replies to posts you've commented on**. Its advice is to keep safety alerts on and turn off digests, trending and reply emails. Each unsubscribe link usually stops only one stream; the category toggles under Settings → Notifications are the complete control. — [Inbox Zero, "How to Stop Nextdoor Emails (2026)"](https://www.getinboxzero.com/blog/post/how-to-stop-nextdoor-emails) (via search excerpt)

### Inferences
- **Post types for TasksNearMe, ranked by fit.** (1) *Ask for a recommendation* ("Who do you recommend for furniture assembly near Mueller?") is Nextdoor's largest category (~30%) and leads straight to bookings. (2) *Task request*, the core transaction. (3) *Thank a helper*, modeled on #ThankANeighbor's themes ("Helped me," "Act of kindness"), limited to people with a completed booking. (4) *Neighborhood tip/question*. Leave out or tightly structure *Safety* and *Lost & found* posts. Those are the categories where Nextdoor ran into profiling and doxxing trouble, and they don't drive marketplace value.
- **Recommendation cards are the bridge from feed to booking.** When someone tags a helper in a recommendation thread, render a card (as Nextdoor did in 2021): helper photo, first name + last initial, verified badge, service area at neighborhood level, completed-task count, recommendation/Fave count, and a "Request" button. Notify the helper, and roll the recommendation up onto their profile.
- **A "Fave" that can't be faked.** Count a Fave only from a verified customer with a completed, paid booking. That is stronger than Nextdoor's claim, and the platform can enforce it. Use Faves as a ranking input for search and recommendation threads, and run per-neighborhood seasonal "Neighborhood Fave" badges with minimum thresholds. Give helpers a shareable Fave link, as Nextdoor gives businesses.
- **Audience controls.** Default to *My neighborhood*, offer *Nearby neighborhoods* and *All of [city]* (the market), and drop Nextdoor's off-platform "Anyone" sharing for any post that names people. If the feed widens distribution by algorithm, as Nextdoor does, only widen posts that carry no personal data, and tell the poster.
- **Feed ranking at launch can be heuristic,** guided by Nextdoor's 2026 direction: locality first (same neighborhood, then nearby), recency decay, conversation over reactions, helper self-promotion pushed down especially at the top, and new users seeing local conversations rather than promotions. Comment notifications and digests can re-engage lapsed users before any ML exists.
- **Threads:** a top-level comment with grouped replies, plus an author-pinned "best answer," which suits recommendation threads well. A distinct "Thanks" reaction (Nextdoor's original) fits a help marketplace better than a generic like.
- **Moderation at launch:** volunteer moderators aren't feasible early, so copy the *structure*, not the volunteers. Use pre-submit friction prompts on comments, reviews and posts, with an optional AI-suggested rewrite (36% edit or withhold, 26% accept the rewrite). Send discrimination and account-level reports to trained staff only. Notify authors which rule applied, and give them an appeal. Watch repeat offenders (<0.5% of users produced ~60% of removals) and send them "Kindness Tips"-style nudges.

## 2. LinkedIn feed: comments and nested replies, reactions, social-proof lines, comment sorting, "see more," notification signals, and endorsement/recommendation trust mechanics

### Takeaway
LinkedIn's patterns that transfer are: a small, labeled reaction set on posts *and* comments; comments ranked per viewer by affinity, freshness, commenter reputation and content quality, with a "Most relevant / Most recent" toggle (and since 2026 a verified-members filter); posting controls (comments off, connections only, block); explicit caps on comment volume tied to verification; per-topic × per-channel notification settings; and a two-tier trust system of approval-gated written recommendations plus lightweight skill endorsements. Its 2026 feed update openly targets engagement pods, comment automation and engagement bait.

### Cited Findings

#### Reactions
- Besides the default **Like**, LinkedIn offers **Celebrate** (milestones, accomplishments), **Support** (empathy or encouragement through a hard time), **Love** (heartwarming, resonates), **Insightful** (a great point, new idea, helpful advice) and **Funny** (humor "in a professional context"). The picker opens by tap-and-hold on mobile or hover on desktop, and you can react to "a post or a comment." — [LinkedIn Help, "Use LinkedIn reactions"](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/101466)
- Third parties disagree on whether "Curious" still exists: GaggleAMP lists seven options including Curious, and SocialPilot says it was removed. LinkedIn's help page lists only the five above plus Like. — [GaggleAMP](https://support.gaggleamp.com/hc/en-us/articles/4411744178963); [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-reactions) (via search excerpts); [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/101466)

#### Comments, replies, sorting and comment controls
- You comment with "Add a comment" and reply under a comment with "Add a reply." Typing **@** mentions someone, and comments can include an emoji, GIF or photo. Comments sort by **Most relevant** or **Most recent** from a dropdown under the comment box, and "Some comments will only be visible under **Most recent** view." — [LinkedIn Help, comments](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/3068)
- **Controls:** members can turn comments off or limit them to connections only, Pages can allow or restrict comments, and members and Pages can block specific commenters. When an author comments on their own post, an **"Author"** tag shows next to the name. — [LinkedIn Help, comments](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/3068)
- **Rate limits:** LinkedIn may cap how many comments a member or Page can make in a set period, based on interactions, blocks and verification status. "If we detect excessive comment creation or use of an automation tool, we may limit the visibility of those comments." — [LinkedIn Help, comments](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/3068)
- **April 2026:** a "Verified Members" filter was added next to Most relevant and Most recent. — [Dataconomy, Apr 27, 2026](https://dataconomy.com/2026/04/27/linkedin-adds-comment-filter-for-verified-users-to-enhance/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-adds-option-to-filter-replies-by-verified-users/818506/) (via search excerpts)

#### How comments are ranked (engineering)
- **Older behavior:** long threads used to sort by recency. A 2016 version ranked by aggregate likes, but it wasn't personalized and favored early comments ("comments would be highly-ranked only after they had garnered enough social proof"). — [LinkedIn Engineering, "Serving top comments in professional social networks," 2017](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/serving-top-comments-in-professional-social-networks)
- **2017 system:** a personalized logistic-regression model predicts how likely *this viewer* is to engage with each comment, using "close to 100 features." Those cover commenter reputation and popularity, **viewer–commenter affinity** (connections, follows, profile similarity, past interactions, shared industry or *location*), content (language, length, structure, hashtags, mentions), engagement, and **freshness** (creation time, last reply, last like), since viewers "have a tendency to read fresh comments." Separate models filter spam and low quality. Comments viewed rose 22% on iOS and about 14% on Android. "Comment Viral Updates," which put a popular post in your feed because a connection commented on it, got 2.5× the interactions per impression of ordinary connection updates. — [same](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/serving-top-comments-in-professional-social-networks)

#### Feed ranking signals and 2026 changes
- **2020:** LinkedIn measures two kinds of dwell time per feed item (time on screen, and time after clicking through) and models the chance a member skips an item. **2021:** passive-consumption signals such as dwell time are objectives in a multi-task ranking utility. — [LinkedIn Engineering, "Understanding feed dwell time," 2020](https://engineering.linkedin.com/blog/2020/understanding-feed-dwell-time); [LinkedIn Engineering, multi-task learning, 2021](https://engineering.linkedin.com/blog/2021/homepage-feed-multi-task-learning-using-tensorflow) (via search excerpts)
- **March 12, 2026 update:** LinkedIn aims to "make engagement pods ineffective and curb comment automation" and to cut unauthorized third-party tools that fake conversations, recycled and click-driven posts, "comment to agree" engagement bait, and videos that don't match their text. When you engage, "you're interacting with a real person and a real point of view." "This isn't about limiting reach or discouraging participation." Ranking now uses "Generative Recommenders" augmented with LLMs, plus profile signals ("your industry, skills, experience, and geography") and how you engage over time. An **Interest Picker** being tested at sign-up (topics such as "Leadership," "Career Growth") addresses the cold start for new members. — [LinkedIn News, "Improving the Feed," Mar 12, 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)
- Coverage says LinkedIn moved away from ranking mostly on *past* engagement toward recent activity and relevance, to keep feeds fresh. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-updates-its-feed-algorithm/814638/) (via search excerpt). Third-party claims about a 150B-parameter "360Brew" ranking model aren't verified against LinkedIn's own posts. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/nicolasai/reverse-engineering-linkedins-360brew-from-their-engineering-blog-4jm6) (via search excerpt)

#### Notifications (activity signals that bring people back)
- In-app notifications appear under the Notifications tab, and email goes to the primary address. They cover reactions to your posts, updates from your network or groups, Pages you follow or manage, and events. Settings → Notifications → topic toggles let you choose delivery per type (in-app, push, email), and some emails have frequency choices. Comment and reaction alerts sit under "Posting and commenting → Comments and reactions," and changes take 48–72 hours to apply. — [LinkedIn Help, "Manage your notification settings"](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1378534) (via search excerpt)
- Third-party tutorials show email-frequency options for comments and reactions such as "Individual," "Weekly" and "LinkedIn recommended." — [Guideflow tutorials](https://www.guideflow.com/tutorial/how-to-set-comments-and-reactions-email-frequency-to-linkedin-recommended-on-linkedin) (third-party, via search listing)

#### Trust mechanics: recommendations and skill endorsements
- **Recommendations** (written) can be requested only from 1st-degree connections you work or have worked with. The recipient can accept, ask for changes, or **hide** one: hidden recommendations are visible to no one and drop out of the displayed count, but can't be deleted by the recipient. A person asked can decline without replying, and the writer can revise or delete their recommendation later. — [LinkedIn Help, accept or dismiss recommendations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/102); [LinkedIn Help, Recommendations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a541653) (via search excerpts)
- **Skill endorsements** need no request. You can hide individual endorsements, show or hide all of them per skill, or opt out entirely, and hiding endorsements can change the order your skills appear in. — [LinkedIn Help, Skill endorsements](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/31888) (via search excerpt)

### Inferences
- **Reaction set for TasksNearMe:** keep it small and in the domain's vocabulary, usable on posts and comments: Like or Heart, **Thanks**, **Helpful** (like Insightful), and perhaps Celebrate for "task done." Skip Funny or other reactions that can read as mockery on someone's request for help. Label each in a tooltip, as LinkedIn's help does.
- **Comment ranking heuristics** taken from LinkedIn's features: affinity becomes *same or nearby neighborhood*, reputation becomes *verified helper with completed tasks or Faves*, plus freshness and a minimum length or quality, with spam filtered separately. Default to "Most relevant" with a "Most recent" toggle, but on a marketplace **don't hide comments** from the relevant view (LinkedIn does). A helper's answer to a request must always be findable, so collapse low-quality comments instead of dropping them.
- **Role tags** like LinkedIn's "Author": show "Poster" and "Verified helper" chips next to names in threads, with first name + last initial and neighborhood only.
- **Two-tier trust:** (1) booking reviews tied to a completed task, which the helper **can't hide**, since LinkedIn-style hiding would bias a marketplace; (2) optional testimonials or recommendations, which the helper approves before they show (as on LinkedIn), clearly labeled as such. Limit "skill endorsements" to customers who booked that service, which avoids no-request endorsement inflation.
- **Notifications** should copy LinkedIn's matrix: per type (reactions, replies to my post or comment, mentions, recommendation requests near me, booking events) × channel (in-app, push, email) × frequency (individual, daily digest, weekly). Booking and safety messages are always on.
- **"A neighbor commented" surfacing** (Comment Viral Updates, 2.5× interactions per impression) suggests a feed card like "2 neighbors in Mueller recommended helpers on this post." Show it only inside the neighborhood audience, and never with street-level detail.

### Gaps
- No official LinkedIn documentation found for the "Name and N others" reaction social-proof line or the reactor list. The pattern is common, but its exact rules aren't sourced here.
- No official source for the "…see more" truncation length (third parties often cite about 3 lines or ~200 characters, but this wasn't verified).
- LinkedIn Help doesn't say whether replies can be nested under replies, or how deep.
- No official LinkedIn page found on mention-notification settings, or on whether reaction types are weighted differently in ranking (third parties disagree).
- The reaction-set history (2019 launch set, later additions or removals) wasn't verified beyond the third-party "Curious" disagreement.

## 3. Mechanics that drive engagement without spam: rate limits, posting prompts, empty states, "trending in your neighborhood"

### Takeaway
Both platforms grow engagement by **lowering the cost of good contributions** (AI prompts, structured composers, pinned answers, celebratory feedback, cold-start interest pickers, re-engagement suggestions, digests) while **raising the cost of bad ones** (comment caps tied to verification, hiding automated comments, downranking self-promotion and engagement bait, pre-post friction prompts). Nextdoor's own data shows the trade-off: friction measurably cuts harmful content (Kindness Reminder 36% edit or withhold; the 2016 forms -75% profiling posts) at some cost to posting volume.

### Cited Findings
- **Rate and volume limits (LinkedIn):** comment caps per member or Page per period, based on interactions, blocks and verification, and reduced visibility for "excessive comment creation or use of an automation tool." — [LinkedIn Help, comments](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/3068)
- **Anti-pod and anti-bait ranking (LinkedIn, Mar 2026):** curbs engagement pods, comment automation, "comment to agree" bait, recycled posts and mismatched videos. — [LinkedIn News, Mar 12, 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed). An earlier update reportedly pushed down engagement-baiting posts and polls. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-updates-feed-algorithm-to-downrank-engagement-baiting-posts-and-po/623313/) (headline, via search listing)
- **Verified-only views (LinkedIn, Apr 2026):** a "Verified Members" comment filter. — [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/04/27/linkedin-adds-comment-filter-for-verified-users-to-enhance/) (via search excerpt)
- **Cold-start prompts:** LinkedIn's Interest Picker at sign-up addresses the problem that new members lack the profile completeness and engagement history the feed relied on. — [LinkedIn News](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed). Nextdoor's 2025 redesign added "AI-generated prompts to spark discussion." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/15/nextdoor-redesigns-app-with-ai-recommendations-local-news-and-real-time-emergency-alerts) (via search excerpt). Nextdoor's AI "Ask" answers recommendation questions from "14 years of neighborhood conversations." — [Meet the New Nextdoor](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/meet-the-new-nextdoor)
- **Re-engagement and quality ranking (Nextdoor, Apr 2026):** ML suggestions for neighbors who haven't visited recently (in early tests they stayed longer and engaged more deeply), less self-promotional or low-quality content "especially at the top," more local conversation for new neighbors, pinned comments, and post-publish celebration animations. — [Nextdoor product updates](https://blog.nextdoor.com/whats-new-on-nextdoor-product-updates-1)
- **Digests and trending:** daily and weekly digests, "trending in your neighborhood" emails, and replies to threads you commented on (third-party description). — [Inbox Zero, 2026](https://www.getinboxzero.com/blog/post/how-to-stop-nextdoor-emails) (via search excerpt)
- **Recognition loops:** #ThankANeighbor themed posts (one got nearly 5,000 reactions) — [Nextdoor blog, Nov 2023](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2023/11/03/nextdoor-thank-a-neighbor). Yearly Fave Awards give winners a badge, a sticker and a spot in a neighborhood hub, and Nextdoor reports that winners see 26× more recommendations. — [2026 Fave Awards release](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoor-opens-voting-for-the-2026-fave-awards)
- **Friction trade-offs:** Kindness Reminder (36% edit or withhold, -15% violating content) — [Nextdoor, Feb 2024](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoors-transparency-report-highlights-impact-of-ai-led-innovation-maintaining-very-low-levels-of-harmful-content-reported-on-the-platform). The 2016 forms carried a "business cost" (less posting) — [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/carolineodonovan/nextdoor-rolls-out-product-fix-it-hopes-will-stem-racial-pro) (via search excerpt).

### Inferences
- **Concrete limits for TasksNearMe:** per-user caps on posts and comments per day that rise with verification and good standing and fall with reports or blocks (LinkedIn's model). Allow one recommendation reply per helper per request, and treat a helper tagging themselves as self-promotion (shown lower, Nextdoor 2026 style). Bar new accounts from posting links for their first N days. Detect duplicate or near-duplicate posts.
- **Empty states with prompts:** a neighborhood feed with little activity should show seeded prompts ("Ask your neighbors who they trust for yard cleanup"), recent completed tasks in anonymized form ("A furniture assembly was completed in Hyde Park this week"), and Faves leaderboards, rather than a blank page.
- **"Trending in your neighborhood":** a digest ranked by replies and saves from distinct neighbors, not raw reactions, with a cap per author, delivered daily or weekly at the user's choice. Helpers get a separate digest, "new recommendation requests you can answer."
- **Visible friction only where it pays:** pre-submit nudges on comments, reviews and any "report a concern" flow. Keep the task-request composer fast, since it is the revenue path.

### Gaps
- No source for Nextdoor's own posting or comment rate limits, duplicate-post rules, or link limits for new accounts.
- No published lift figures for Nextdoor's digests, trending emails, AI discussion prompts, or the reported drop in posting after the 2016 forms.
- No official LinkedIn numbers on the impact of the 2026 anti-pod changes.

## 4. Criticisms a local marketplace should avoid (negativity, privacy leaks, doxxing of addresses, bias) and the safeguards these platforms use

### Takeaway
Nextdoor's documented failures were **racial profiling in safety posts, police-forwarding features that amplified profiling, volunteer moderators removing racial-justice posts or showing bias, over-moderation errors, incivility concentrated in a small group of commenters, and confusion about address and name display**. Its fixes were structured composers, pre-post prompts, staff-only handling of sensitive reports, consensus voting with no power over accounts, appeals, transparency reports, and dropping features like Forward to Police. LinkedIn's main problem is inauthentic engagement (pods, automation, bait), which it handles with comment caps, visibility limits, verification filters and comment controls.

### Cited Findings
- **Racial profiling:** before 2016, safety posts led to profiling. Nextdoor's guided form with a "higher bar" for race as a descriptor cut profiling posts by 75% in test markets, according to the company. — [CBS News, Sept 2016](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nextdoor-social-network-neighborhoods-racial-profiling-decline-after-changes). One user still reported seeing profiling after the rollout. — [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/carolineodonovan/nextdoor-rolls-out-product-fix-it-hopes-will-stem-racial-pro) (via search excerpt)
- **Forward to Police** (introduced 2016) let users re-send posts to participating law-enforcement agencies. Nextdoor removed it in June 2020 as part of its anti-racism work, after critics argued it enabled profiling, and also cited low use by agencies. — [Engadget, June 2020](https://www.engadget.com/nextdoor-pulls-forward-to-police-002837345.html); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/nextdoor-drops-forward-to-police-feature-after-rampant-1844106727) (via search excerpts)
- **Moderator bias (2020):** after volunteer moderators removed Black Lives Matter posts, the CEO publicly took the blame ("It's our fault"). — [WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/post/its-our-fault-nextdoor-ceo-takes-blame-censorship-black-lives-matter-posts) (headline). A petition targeted Nextdoor's "moderator problem." — [Tech.co, June 2020](https://tech.co/news/nextdoor-moderators-racism-petition-2020-06) (headline). The safeguards that followed: racism and misinformation reports go only to trained staff, and volunteers can't see who reported or act on accounts — [Nextdoor blog, Feb 2021](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2021/02/10/how-moderation-works-on-nextdoor); the CEO committed to diversifying moderators — [Engadget, Apr 2021](https://www.engadget.com/nextdoor-anti-racism-notification-180204693.html); decisions require consensus rather than one moderator, and privileges are removed for a pattern of bad votes — [Nextdoor, How to moderate](https://go.us.nextdoor.com/learn-about-moderation) (via search excerpt).
- **Over-moderation:** Nextdoor removed a July 4 post that said "God Bless America" and blamed the comment section. — [WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/nation-world/nextdoor-app-removes-july-4th-post-that-said-god-bless-america-for-discrimination-spam-independence-day-fourth-social-media) (headline). Appeals were expanded in 2023. — [Nextdoor, Feb 2024](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoors-transparency-report-highlights-impact-of-ai-led-innovation-maintaining-very-low-levels-of-harmful-content-reported-on-the-platform)
- **Negativity is concentrated:** "less than half a percent" of neighbors produce "almost 60% of removed content, mainly in comments." Safeguards: Kindness Reminder (with AI rewrite) and Kindness Tips for repeat offenders. — [Nextdoor, Feb 2024](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoors-transparency-report-highlights-impact-of-ai-led-innovation-maintaining-very-low-levels-of-harmful-content-reported-on-the-platform)
- **Privacy, addresses and names:** guides conflict on whether Nextdoor shows the full address or the street only by default, and warn that post content can reveal an exact location even when the address is hidden. — [PurePrivacy](https://www.pureprivacy.com/blog/how-to/remove-address-from-nextdoor/); [PrivacyOn, 2026](https://www.privacyon.com/blog/how-to-protect-your-privacy-on-nextdoor) (via search excerpts). "Anyone"-audience posts can be shared off Nextdoor — [Nextdoor blog, Oct 2020](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2020/10/20/new-features-help-you-stay-connected-to-more-of-the-neighborhoods-you-care-about). Real-name policy plus a choice of how much of the name shows — [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/nextdoor/nextdoor-privacy-policy/user-controls-degree-of-profile-information-sharing/). Stronger fake-name enforcement in 2026 — [Nextdoor product updates](https://blog.nextdoor.com/whats-new-on-nextdoor-product-updates-1).
- **Household-level targeting** of alerts (power outage sent only to affected homes) shows Nextdoor uses exact addresses on the server side without showing them. — [TechCrunch, July 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/15/nextdoor-redesigns-app-with-ai-recommendations-local-news-and-real-time-emergency-alerts) (via search excerpt)
- **Commercialization backlash:** Nextdoor itself now pushes self-promotional posts down — [Nextdoor, Apr 2026](https://blog.nextdoor.com/whats-new-on-nextdoor-product-updates-1). A third-party review complains of a paywall for service businesses commenting on requests — [Unstar, 2026](https://unstar.app/blog/nextdoor-business-paywall-cant-comment-suspended-2026) (unverified).
- **Inauthentic engagement (LinkedIn):** pods, automation and bait are targeted. Comment caps and visibility limits apply to automation, and commenters can be restricted to connections or blocked. — [LinkedIn News, Mar 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed); [LinkedIn Help, comments](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/3068)

### Inferences
- **Never show exact addresses in social surfaces.** Use them server-side for targeting only (the way Nextdoor alerts reach households). Publicly show the neighborhood name and, at most, an approximate map area. Reveal the exact address only to the booked helper after confirmation. In any post, comment or review composer, detect street numbers, unit numbers, phone numbers and gate codes, and show a Kindness-Reminder-style prompt ("Remove your address? Neighbors don't need it to recommend a helper").
- **Names:** first name + last initial everywhere social, with no setting that shows full names to the neighborhood. That is stricter than Nextdoor's real-name display, and it fits a marketplace where helpers enter strangers' homes.
- **No public safety or crime post type, and no forwarding to police.** If users can report a concern about a person, use a private, structured, staff-only form that asks for *behavior* (what happened, when, which booking). If a protected characteristic is mentioned, apply Nextdoor's 2016 "higher bar" logic.
- **Moderation structure:** staff handle discrimination reports and anything that touches an account. Tell authors which rule they broke, offer an appeal, and publish a short yearly transparency note (reports per 1,000 posts, median removal time, share of edits after prompts), as Nextdoor does, which builds trust.
- **Helpers aren't advertisers.** Rank helpers in recommendation threads by verified Faves and completed tasks, not payment. Avoid any "pay to reply to requests" mechanic, which is the source of the backlash reported against Nextdoor.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-company) evaluation of Nextdoor's 75% profiling reduction, the Kindness Reminder effect, or the anti-racism notification beyond the company's own figures and a third-party summary of 2021 edit rates.
- No documented incidents of doxxing via Nextdoor addresses were researched in depth here; the privacy findings above are about settings and display ambiguity, not specific incidents.
- No studies on the "negativity/fear" effect of Nextdoor safety content were retrieved, and no data on bias in Nextdoor's Faves or recommendations, for example by neighborhood demographics.
