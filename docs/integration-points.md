# Integration points: from stand-ins to real providers

Every outside service sits behind a small interface in `src/integrations/*` (or, for verification, in `src/modules/verification`). Development and tests use stand-ins with the same interface, so the app, its tests and the e2e flows run with no accounts or keys. This page lists each one, what the real adapter must do, and what to check when you switch.

**The safety rule in the code:** when a provider's key is set but its adapter isn't written yet, the factory throws instead of quietly faking. In production builds, the payment, gift card and AI stand-ins return `null` (the feature is unavailable) instead of pretending.

## What each adapter must do

### Payments (Stripe)
**Built** (`src/integrations/payments/stripe.ts`, REST without the SDK): on when both `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` and `STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` are set. With the secret key alone (Stripe Identity only), payments keep the development stand-in outside production; production without both takes no payments.
- **New cards** come from Stripe's own card field (`StripeCardField`, Stripe.js loaded from js.stripe.com): once complete, it becomes a PaymentMethod (`pm_…`) and the checkout sends `{ kind: 'stripe_card', paymentMethodId, save }`. The server checks the brand (Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover) and attaches the card to the person's Customer (`payment_customers`, migration 0038; made the first time), so later charges on the same booking (changes, counter-offers) and saved cards work. "Save this card" only decides whether it's listed for next time. Removing a saved card detaches it.
- `charge`: a PaymentIntent confirmed at once, on the person's customer, with the app's idempotency key (one per attempt; T-05). `authorize` / `capture` / `cancelAuthorization`: manual-capture intents for direct invites (card holds last 7 days at most). `refund`: partial and full refunds with the given key; a pending refund counts as done.
- **3-D Secure:** an intent that needs the bank's step comes back as `requires_bank_verification` with `{ kind: 'stripe', clientSecret, publishableKey }`; the bank dialog runs `stripe.handleNextAction` and then calls the same `onFinishVerification`, whose `completeAction` reads the outcome from Stripe (the browser's word is never trusted).
- **Webhooks:** `/api/webhooks/stripe` (signed with `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`) also takes `payment_intent.succeeded`, `.amount_capturable_updated`, `.payment_failed` and `.canceled`. They find the payment by its intent and run the same finish functions as the browser (bookings, changes, invite holds and counter-offers), which act only on a payment still waiting, so duplicates and events out of order are harmless. A payment that succeeded after its hold ran out is settled anyway and goes to support for a refund (`payment.refund_needed`).
- Card declines map to `payment_declined` / `insufficient_funds`; Stripe errors that aren't the card's (5xx, timeouts) are thrown and retried by the person.

### Transfers and payouts (Stripe Connect)
**Built** (`src/integrations/payments/stripe-connect.ts`, `src/modules/payments/connect.ts`): on with the Stripe payment keys.
- **Payout accounts:** each helper gets an Express account (US, individual, transfers) and finishes Stripe's onboarding through an account link that comes back to the payout step; "Check status" reads the account again. `account.updated` sets `payout_accounts.status` (active once transfers and payouts are enabled; requirements due; restricted when Stripe disables it). A development account is replaced the first time a helper starts with Stripe.
- **Transfers:** with Stripe, earnings, reimbursements, cancellation compensation and tips are recorded `scheduled` (inside the booking's transaction), and the `earnings.transfers` job moves each as its own Transfer once the helper's account is active, tied to the booking's charge (`source_transaction`) when the charge covers it, with `transfer:<earnings id>` as the idempotency key. Rows that can't move yet stay scheduled and are retried. Use `recordEarning()` for any new kind of money to a helper.
- **Payouts to the bank:** Stripe pays connected accounts on its own schedule. Point a Connect webhook (events on connected accounts) at `/api/webhooks/stripe` with `account.updated` and `payout.created`, `.paid`, `.failed` and `.canceled`, and put its signing secret in `STRIPE_CONNECT_WEBHOOK_SECRET`. A paid payout marks the helper's earlier transfers paid out. The development simulation of payouts stays off with Stripe.
- **Instant payouts** (P2): a helper with an active account can cash out what Stripe says is instantly available (`/v1/balance` on the connected account) to their debit card in minutes (`/v1/payouts`, `method: instant`), up to 3 times a day. The fee is theirs: 1.5%, at least $0.50 (`INSTANT_PAYOUT_RULES`). Set the same Instant Payouts fee in Stripe's Connect pricing settings so Stripe takes it from the payout; otherwise the platform pays Stripe's 1%. The payout's events mark the earnings paid out. Development pays out the transferred money at once.
- **The payout dashboard:** "Manage payouts" opens the helper's Express Dashboard through a one-time login link (payouts, bank details, tax forms), and the payout setup step before the account is active.

### ID checks (Persona, or Stripe Identity) and background checks (Checkr)
- **Built** against the providers' REST APIs, without SDKs; with their keys (test keys in development) they replace the stand-ins on their own. Production needs every verification provider, so it waits for the Stripe Connect payout adapter. The rules stay in `src/modules/verification` (`standing.ts`, `progress.ts`; starting and recording in `tracks.ts`, staff decisions in `staff.ts`, timers in `timers.ts`), and the webhooks go through the same recorders the dev pages use (`webhooks.ts`).
- **Which ID provider:** Persona when its three settings are set, otherwise Stripe Identity when `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` is, otherwise the stand-in (outside production only). Production needs one of the two. Each row keeps its provider (`dev`, `stripe_identity`, `persona`), and a helper's provider check is the double check after our own photos are approved (plan 6.1); a client's risk-triggered check is the provider's alone.
- **Persona** (plan 6.2, the owner's choice): `POST /api/v1/inquiries` with `Authorization: Bearer`, a pinned `Persona-Version` (2026-09-29), `Key-Inflection: kebab` and an `Idempotency-Key` (one per person per minute). The body names the template (`inquiry-template-id`), the `redirect-uri` (the helper's ID step, or the client's ID page with where to go next), and in `meta` our user id as `auto-create-account-reference-id` (the only thing we send about the person) with `auto-create-one-time-link`. The person goes to `meta["one-time-link"]`; the inquiry id (`inq_…`) is `provider_session_id`. Webhooks at `/api/webhooks/persona` carry `Persona-Signature`: one or more space-separated `t=<unix>,v1=<hex>` sets (two while a secret rotates), each an HMAC-SHA256 of `t.<raw body>` with the endpoint's secret; any matching set, compared in constant time and at most 5 minutes old, is accepted. Subscribe to `inquiry.completed`, `.approved`, `.declined`, `.failed`, `.expired` and `.marked-for-review`. The event's inquiry (`data.attributes.payload.data`) is matched by our stored inquiry id, never the deprecated reference id. Events arrive out of order and more than once: each row keeps the creation time of the newest event applied (`provider_event_at`) and Persona's word for it (`provider_outcome`, shown to staff), an older or repeated event changes nothing, a verified result is final, and a progress update never undoes a decline. `approved` verifies (the name and birth date of the passed government ID verification in `included` are compared and checked once, never stored), `declined` and `failed` need a new try, `expired` closes the session, anything else is in progress. Redaction 30 days after a final result: `DELETE /api/v1/inquiries/<id>`.
- **When the double check disagrees with our team** (plan 6.2): a decline or failure after staff approved a helper's photos pauses the helper (`double_check_review`) and opens a case in the identity queue with both results (when our team approved, and Persona's outcome). Staff let them try the double check again (our approval stands) or turn the photos down (`double_check_failed`: new photos, within the limits); either way the note goes to the audit log with both results, and the helper is told on the ID step. Our photos are deleted once both stages are final (the double check cleared the ID), with the 30-day timer as the backstop.
- **Stripe Identity:** a VerificationSession with `type=document`, `require_matching_selfie`, `require_live_capture`, driving licence, ID card or passport, and `return_url` back to the identity step (one per person per minute through `Idempotency-Key`). Webhooks (`identity.verification_session.processing`, `.verified`, `.requires_input`, `.canceled`) are checked against `Stripe-Signature` (HMAC-SHA256 of `t.payload`, 5 minutes). For a verified session the app reads `verified_outputs` and `verified_outputs.dob` once: the name is compared with the account's, the age checked (under 18 closes helper onboarding), and neither is stored. `last_error.code` becomes what the person can do next. Redaction 30 days after a final result calls `/redact`.
- **Checkr:** a candidate (email, name, our user id as `custom_id`, work location Austin, TX) and an invitation for `CHECKR_PACKAGE`; the candidate id is the check's reference. Webhooks are checked against `X-Checkr-Signature` (HMAC-SHA256 of the body, keyed with the API key): `invitation.completed` (the report is underway), `invitation.expired`, `report.completed` (clear or consider), `report.suspended`, `report.resumed`, `report.canceled`. Staff's pre-adverse decision creates Checkr's adverse action on the latest report with every adverse item, and Checkr sends the final notice 7 days later on its own; if staff decide for the person in between, the adverse action is canceled.
- Store only what the app uses: "verified", "is adult", the name match, and the check's status. Never the date of birth, the document, or report contents.

### Phone and home address
- **Phone:** the onboarding step uses the same texted code as the account page (`src/modules/accounts/phone-verification.ts`), sent through Twilio when its keys are set (below). A number Twilio can't text (a landline, an invalid number, one that replied STOP) comes back to the person as an invalid number.
- **Home address:** chosen with the address search (Mapbox with `MAPBOX_TOKEN`, the sample addresses otherwise) and confirmed again on submit, so only real address points can be sent. The proof of address is stored with the app's file storage (local disk now; the R2 adapter must keep `address_proof/` objects private, with no public URL). Staff open it at `/api/verification-documents/<token>`.

### Passkeys
- **Built in** (Better Auth's passkey plugin, `@better-auth/passkey` with SimpleWebAuthn; nothing to buy). People add a passkey on the account page ("Passkeys": a name, the device's own prompt) and then use "Sign in with a passkey" on the email step. The email code always keeps working. The relying party is `APP_URL`'s host, so passkeys made on one domain don't work on another: set `APP_URL` to the real domain before people add them. Credentials live in `passkeys` (public keys only).
- A suspended or deleted account gets no session by any route (a `session.create` guard in `src/platform/auth/config.ts`).

### Sign-in with Google or Microsoft
There's no adapter to write: Better Auth has both providers built in. With keys set, the real provider replaces the stand-in for that provider; production builds without keys don't show its button.
- **Google** (Google Cloud console → APIs & Services → Credentials): an OAuth client of type *Web application*. Authorized redirect URI: `<APP_URL>/api/auth/callback/google` (one per environment). The consent screen asks only for `openid`, `email` and `profile`. Publish the consent screen before launch, or only test users can sign in.
- **Microsoft** (Microsoft Entra admin center → App registrations): supported account types *Accounts in any organizational directory and personal Microsoft accounts* (tenant `common`, the default) or *Personal Microsoft accounts only* (`MICROSOFT_TENANT_ID=consumers`). Platform *Web*, redirect URI `<APP_URL>/api/auth/callback/microsoft`, and a client secret (note its expiry date). To let work and school accounts sign up, add the optional ID-token claim `xms_edov` (email domain owner verified) under Token configuration. Without it, only personal accounts (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live) and work accounts that say `email_verified` can create or join an account.
- **The rules, whichever provider:** an email creates or joins an account only when the provider confirms it (Google's `email_verified`; a Microsoft personal account, or `xms_edov`). Otherwise an organization's admin could claim anyone's address ("nOAuth"). Suspended, banned and closed accounts can't sign in this way. The account keeps "First L." and never takes the provider's photo (people add their own on the account page). Provider tokens are encrypted at rest, and no refresh tokens or Graph access are requested. Connecting and disconnecting go in the audit log.
- **To check when you switch:** sign in as a new person (the profile step shows their name), as an existing person with the same email (it joins their account), cancel on the provider's page (the sign-in page says so), then connect and disconnect the other provider from the account page.
- **"Error 400: redirect_uri_mismatch"** on Google's page means the OAuth client doesn't list the exact callback: add `<APP_URL>/api/auth/callback/google` (in development `http://localhost:3000/api/auth/callback/google`) under Authorized redirect URIs; Google can take a few minutes to apply it. While the consent screen is in Testing, only the test users listed there can sign in.
- **Tests never use the real providers:** outside production the stand-in also sits beside a provider that has keys, and answers the sign-ins of a browser with the `tnm_oauth_stand_in=1` cookie (the e2e tests set it).

### Email (Postmark or Resend) and texts (Twilio)
- **Postmark is built.** With `POSTMARK_SERVER_TOKEN` and `EMAIL_FROM` set, every email goes out through Postmark's transactional stream with its plain-text and designed versions and a tag (`sign-in-code`, `email-change-code`, `email-changed`, `phone-changed`, `welcome`, …). `EMAIL_FROM` must be a sender signature or a domain verified in Postmark (DKIM and Return-Path records). Until Postmark approves the account, it only delivers to addresses on your own verified domain.
- **Resend is built too**, the quickest way to see the emails in a real inbox. With `RESEND_API_KEY` and `EMAIL_FROM=onboarding@resend.dev`, Resend delivers only to the address that owns the Resend account; verify a domain in Resend (its DNS records) and set `EMAIL_FROM` on it to send to anyone. The tag goes along as a `kind` tag. Setting both keys is refused at startup.
- The designed emails share one layout (`src/platform/email/layout.ts`): tables and inline styles, light and dark, hover where supported. Every email uses it: the sign-in and email-change codes, the change notices and the welcome email (`src/modules/accounts/emails/`), every notification (`src/modules/notifications/emails.ts`: the topic, what happened, a next step, a button to the page) and the gift card email (`src/modules/rewards/emails.ts`). Each email also has a plain-text version.
- **Outside production a key never takes the dev outbox away:** every email is recorded there (e2e tests read codes from it), and only real addresses also go out. Reserved test domains (`example.test`, `.example`, `example.com`, …) never reach a provider in any environment. Before a domain is verified, a refusal from the provider is logged, not thrown.
- **Unsubscribe:** notification emails in optional topics (offers, messages, tips and news) carry a signed unsubscribe link and the one-click `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers (RFC 8058, required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders). Mail apps post to `/api/email/unsubscribe`; the link in the footer opens `/email/unsubscribe`, which asks first. Booking and payment emails are receipts and safety updates and can't be turned off.
- **Bounces and complaints:** in Resend, add a webhook to `<APP_URL>/api/webhooks/resend` for `email.bounced` and `email.complained`, and put its signing secret in `RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET`. A permanent bounce or a complaint adds the address to `email_suppressions`; notification email then skips it (`email_suppressed` on the staff person page). Sign-in codes still go out. `liftEmailSuppression` lets support undo it. Postmark's bounce webhook is still to do.
- **Retries:** a notification email is sent with its delivery id as the idempotency key, so a retry after a timeout never sends twice (Resend).
- **To send to everyone:** verify the domain in Resend (its SPF and DKIM records, plus a DMARC record), set `EMAIL_FROM` to an address on it (e.g. `TasksNearMe <hello@yourdomain>`), and set `EMAIL_REPLY_TO` to a support inbox.
- `send` is all the app needs. Notification emails and texts go through `deliverNotifications` (`src/modules/notifications/delivery.ts`), which records every attempt in `notification_deliveries` and retries failures 5, 30 and 120 minutes later. Throw on a failed send so it's retried.
- Texts go only to verified phones; texts that can wait are held from 9 pm to 8 am market time.
- **Twilio is built** (`src/integrations/sms/twilio.ts`, REST without the SDK): one message per text from the Messaging Service (`TWILIO_MESSAGING_SERVICE_SID`, preferred) or `TWILIO_FROM`. The app makes and checks its own six-digit codes, so Twilio only delivers them. Outside production every text is also kept in `/dev/outbox`, and numbers in the 555 exchange (the seeded and test people) never reach Twilio; production without the keys refuses to send rather than drop texts.
- Before texting real people: register the sender (A2P 10DLC or toll-free). Twilio answers STOP, HELP and START itself on a Messaging Service; a number that replied STOP is refused (error 21610) and shows as one we can't text.

### Gift cards (Tremendous)
- **Tremendous is built** (`src/integrations/gift-cards/tremendous.ts`, REST without the SDK): `order` places one order per grant with the grant id as `external_id` (idempotent at Tremendous: a retry returns the first order), LINK delivery from the campaign in `TREMENDOUS_CAMPAIGN_ID` (its catalog, branding and message), paid from `TREMENDOUS_FUNDING_SOURCE_ID` (default `BALANCE`, the prepaid balance), and the helper's name and email as the recipient. Sandbox keys (`TEST_…`) use testflight.tremendous.com. The link replaces `/dev/gift-cards/<id>`.
- A refused order (funding, the campaign) or one waiting for approval in Tremendous has no link yet: the grant shows as failed and staff reissue it from the rewards queue after fixing or approving it there (same `external_id`, so never twice).

### Address search (Mapbox)
- **Built.** With `MAPBOX_TOKEN` (a public `pk.` token; don't add URL restrictions to it, since the app calls Mapbox from its server), the posting wizard's "Search your address" uses Mapbox's Geocoding API v6: US addresses, nearest the market first, within twice its radius. Temporary results are free up to 100,000 requests a month; each search is one request, and people are limited to 120 searches per 10 minutes.
- **Only real addresses:** suggestions are known address points (Mapbox's `rooftop`, `parcel` or `point` accuracy). Results Mapbox only guesses along a street (`interpolated`) aren't offered, since their house number may not exist. A new home Mapbox doesn't know yet can't be chosen until Mapbox adds it.
- **Confirmed on "Post":** `resolve` looks the chosen address up again as a full address. The same house number and street, in the same city or ZIP, must come back as a known address point with exact or high confidence. The address is saved as Mapbox has it, so a wrong ZIP or a variant spelling ("W. Second St") is corrected. If Mapbox can't be reached, posting fails with a "try again" message and nothing is saved.
- **Mapbox's terms** (Product Terms 2.7.2): temporary results may not be stored or cached, and their coordinates may not be shown. The coordinates are used once on the server to find the service area and neighborhood, then dropped. The browser never gets them. The place id it holds is the address itself, signed with `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, so `resolve` takes only addresses this app suggested. What's stored is the address (encrypted), its ZIP and its area; the public point is fuzzed from the area's center, as before.
- **Not UPS or USPS:** their address validation may be used only for parcels shipped or mail sent with them (UPS: "solely for the purpose of validating an address in connection with tendering a package"), so neither can check addresses here. If a second opinion is ever wanted, Geocodio's free tier (2,500 lookups a day, results may be stored) is allowed.
- **Tests never call Mapbox:** a browser with the `tnm_geo_stand_in=1` cookie gets the fictional sample addresses (never in production), as `tests/e2e/posting.spec.ts` does. Outside production the sample place ids (`plc_…`) still resolve beside Mapbox. A production build without a token has no address search rather than fictional addresses.
- Only the public point (fuzzed 300–800 m) and the area are ever shown to anyone but the client and the booked helper.

### File storage (R2)
- **R2 is built** (`src/integrations/storage/r2.ts`): `put`, `get` and `delete` through R2's S3-compatible API, signed with AWS Signature Version 4 by Node's crypto (checked against the AWS documentation's example). Create a bucket with no public access and an R2 API token with Object Read & Write on it; its S3 access key pair goes in `R2_ACCESS_KEY_ID` / `R2_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`, the Cloudflare account id in `R2_ACCOUNT_ID`. Every read still goes through the app (`/api/files/<id>`, `/api/verification-documents/<token>`), which checks who may see the file, so the bucket never needs a public URL.
- Production without R2 refuses to store files (rather than keep them on a disk the next deploy wipes); `FILE_STORAGE=local` keeps the local disk on a server whose disk lasts.
- `put` and `get` by key. Uploads already strip metadata (EXIF, GPS) and re-encode before storing; with R2, switch to presigned direct uploads so photos don't pass through Server Actions (decisions.md).
- Profile photos (`kind: avatar`) are cropped to a 512 px square around the face or subject. One is public only while it's its owner's current photo (`isCurrentProfilePhoto`); a replaced or removed photo goes back to owner and staff only. With a CDN in front of R2, keep avatars out of long shared caches, or a removed photo stays visible until the cache expires.

### Writing help (Claude)
- **Built.** With `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, three helpers use Claude through the official SDK (`@anthropic-ai/sdk`, pinned): "Help me describe this" when posting (`task-drafter.ts`), and "Write it for me" on a helper's profile (`profile-writer.ts`) and offer message (`offer-writer.ts`). Each call asks for one structured answer that matches a zod schema (`messages.parse`), then the app tidies it to the form's rules (allowed detail values, length limits) and masks contact details. Claude Opus 5.5 at low effort by default, about 4 to 5 seconds a draft; `ANTHROPIC_MODEL=claude-haiku-5-5` is cheaper and faster.
- **What Claude sees:** only what the feature needs. The poster's typed text and category; a helper's first name, services, area, tools, languages, notes and current profile; for an offer, the task as that helper sees it (contact details already masked, never the address) and their own profile. No last names, emails, phone numbers or addresses are sent on purpose; typed text passes through as written.
- **Rules around every call:** the market's `ai_drafting` flag (one switch for all three), an hourly limit per person (10 task drafts, 10 profile drafts, 20 offer messages), prohibited phrases checked before calling, and a refusal from Claude reads as `prohibited`. Drafts save nothing.
- **Tests never call Claude:** without a key, development and tests use the stand-ins; where a key is set, a browser with the `tnm_ai_stand_in=1` cookie gets the stand-ins (never in production), as the e2e tests do.
- Before launch: list Anthropic as a subprocessor in the privacy policy, and watch the per-person limits against real use.

### Web push (notifications on a device)
- **Built.** A device turns notifications on from the account page ("Notifications on this device"), after posting a task, or at the end of helper setup; the browser asks only after that tap. `public/sw.js` shows each push and opens its page. Subscriptions are `push_subscriptions` (one per browser); a subscription the push service says is gone is deleted.
- **What's sent:** every notification the inbox gets, except tips and news, with the same quiet hours as texts (urgent day-of updates go at once). The message is encrypted for the browser (RFC 8291), so the push service can't read it; the request is signed with our VAPID key (RFC 8292).
- **Only real push services:** the server posts to the endpoint a browser hands over, so only Google's, Mozilla's, Apple's and Windows' push hosts are accepted.
- **iPhone:** web push works only from a Home Screen app (iOS 16.4+), which the manifest makes installable; the account page explains this on iPhone Safari.
- Before launch: a production key pair in the host's secrets (never `.env.local`), and `WEB_PUSH_SUBJECT` set to a real `mailto:` or `https:` contact.

### Bot checks (Cloudflare Turnstile)
- **Built** (`src/integrations/turnstile/turnstile.ts`, REST without an SDK; blueprint 07 §2 and §8, 06 §4). With both keys, the email step (asking for a sign-in code, action `sign_in`), the name step that finishes sign-up (`sign_up`, which also covers Google and Microsoft sign-ups) and "Post" on a new task (`post_task`) show Cloudflare's widget. It asks for a click only when Cloudflare isn't sure (`appearance: interaction-only`), and its answer goes with the form as `botCheck`.
- **Checked on the server, once per answer:** `POST https://challenges.cloudflare.com/turnstile/v0/siteverify` with the secret, the answer, the person's address and an idempotency key. The answer must come from the same form (`action`). Answers last 300 seconds and work once, so the widget gets a new one after every submit. A turned-down answer comes back as `fieldErrors.botCheck` (`invalid`, or `expired` for a used or timed-out one), and the form says so by the widget.
- **When Cloudflare is down** (`internal-error`, a 5xx, a timeout after 5 seconds): one retry with the same idempotency key, then the request goes through and a warning is logged. The rate limits still apply, and an outage at Cloudflare shouldn't stop every sign-in (decisions.md, 2026-11-13). A wrong secret (`invalid-input-secret`) throws instead, so it shows in the logs.
- **Production without keys refuses those three forms** (the person sees "something went wrong"), like texts and files: the stand-in never runs in a production build. Setting one key without the other is a setup error (thrown on the first check).
- **Setting it up:** in the Cloudflare dashboard, Turnstile → Add widget, mode *Managed*, hostnames: the real domain (and `localhost` for trying it in development). Put the site key in `TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` and the secret key in `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY`. The widget's script and frame load from `challenges.cloudflare.com`, which the Content Security Policy allows (`src/shared/csp.ts`).
- **Tests never call Cloudflare:** without keys, development and tests use the stand-in; where keys are set, a browser with the `tnm_turnstile_stand_in=1` cookie gets the stand-in (never in production).
- **Offers** run the same check (action `offer`): sending an offer, the one-tap offer on find work (one widget for the list, a fresh answer after each tap) and a counter-offer to an invite. Revising an offer doesn't.

### Reading proofs of address (Claude)
- **Built.** Each proof of address waiting for review is read once by the job runner (two a minute, after every other job, since it waits on the network): the photo (stored as WebP) or PDF goes to Claude with the account's name and the address typed in, and Claude answers five questions in a fixed shape (`messages.parse`): readable, name matches, address matches, dated within 90 days, and the kind of document. Only those answers are stored, in `address_verifications.auto_check`, with the reader's name; never the document's text. Staff see them beside the proof and still decide.
- **Who gets the stand-in:** accounts on reserved domains (seeded people and tests, such as `example.test`), and development without a key. In production without a key nothing is read, and staff review as before.
- **Failures:** a refusal or an error stores "unreadable" with `(unavailable)` beside the reader's name, and the proof is never retried, so a bad document can't loop. The address step tells people that an automated reader checks their document first.
- Before launch: the same subprocessor listing as writing help, and a look at a sample of readings against staff decisions.

### Reading IDs (Claude, our own ID check)
- **Built** (plan 6.1). Each capture waiting for review is read once by the same job: only the ID's front (stored encrypted, decrypted in memory for the reading) goes to Claude with the declared kind of ID, and Claude transcribes the name, birth date, expiry date, kind and issuing state in a fixed shape (`beta.messages.parse`, with `fallbacks: "default"` for a declined request). The app keeps only yes/no answers (the name matches the account's legal name, 18 or older, not expired), the expiry month and year, and the kind, in `identity_captures.auto_check`; never the transcription. The selfie is never sent and faces are never compared: staff do that, then the provider double-checks.
- **Stand-ins and failures** as for proofs of address: reserved domains and development without a key get the stand-in; production without a key reads nothing; a refusal or an error stores "unreadable" and is never retried.
- Before launch: the subprocessor listing covers IDs too, and a look at a sample of readings against staff decisions.

## Switching a provider on

1. Add the test-mode keys to `.env.local` (never commit them).
2. Write the adapter behind the existing interface and remove the factory's `throw`.
3. Run `node scripts/gate.mjs`: the unit and integration tests keep using the stand-ins, so they stay deterministic. Add an adapter test against the provider's test mode.
4. Walk the flow by hand in test mode: booking with a 3-D Secure card, a refund, a payout, an ID check, a gift card.
5. Check the launch scenarios that needed the real provider (blueprint 10 §3.2: T-03 with real cards, webhooks out of order, provider timeouts).
