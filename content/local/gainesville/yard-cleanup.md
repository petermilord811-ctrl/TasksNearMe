Yard cleanup stays at ground level: raking and bagging leaves, weeding, picking up debris and light hand pruning. Ladders, chainsaws and tree work aren't part of it, and neither is hauling debris away, which is where curbside collection comes in.

## Oak leaves in late winter and spring

Gainesville's live oaks and laurel oaks drop most of their old leaves from late winter into spring, as the new ones come in, along with a coat of yellow pollen. Pine needles come down too, mostly in the fall. So the big cleanup season here is spring, not only autumn.

## Bagging for the city

If the City of Gainesville collects your trash, yard waste is picked up every week on your garbage day. Use paper yard-waste bags or a reusable container, never plastic bags, keep each one under 40 pounds, and set them out within 4 feet of the curb. Rules change from time to time, so check the city's [yard waste collection](https://www.gainesvillefl.gov/Government-Pages/Government/Departments/Public-Works/Garbage-Recycling/Curbside-Collection-Services/Yard-Waste-Collection) page before a big cleanup, and say in your task whether you have bags and where they should go. Alachua County and the towns around Gainesville have their own rules.

## Heat, storms and timing

From June to September, early mornings are the best time for outdoor work, before the afternoon storms. Mention shade, where to find water, and any pets in the yard.
