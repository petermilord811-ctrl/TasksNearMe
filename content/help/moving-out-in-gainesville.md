Gainesville moves in waves. Many leases near the University of Florida run out at the end of July, a couple of weeks before new ones start in August, and the weeks around fall finals in December bring another round of move-outs. Here's what to plan, with the City's own rules, and the parts a helper on TasksNearMe can take on.

## Two or three weeks before

- **Book help early.** The busiest weeks fill up. Post your task with your date window, what's moving, the floors and stairs, and whether a truck is waiting: [moving help](/gainesville/moving-help) covers the lifting, loading and carrying, not the vehicle.
- **Read the move-out part of your lease:** the cleaning it expects, how keys go back and when the inspection is.
- **Plan your utilities.** If Gainesville Regional Utilities serves your home, stop or move your service at [gru.com](https://www.gru.com/) before your last day.

## Furniture you're not taking

- **A house with the City of Gainesville's curbside service:** the City picks up bulk items for free, with no appointment, on Tuesdays, Wednesdays and Thursdays. Put them within 4 feet of the curb by 7 a.m. on your collection day, at least 4 feet from your carts. Furniture, mattresses and box springs, grills and exercise equipment are accepted. Appliances and electronics need an appointment (the City's Solid Waste Division, 352-334-2330), and pianos, hot tubs, tires, paint and propane tanks aren't collected. Check the City's [bulk item collection](https://www.gainesvillefl.gov/Government-Pages/Government/Departments/Public-Works/Garbage-Recycling/Curbside-Collection-Services/Bulk-Item-Collection) page before you set anything out.
- **An apartment or condo:** the City's bulk pickup is only for homes in its residential curbside program, so ask your community's office how furniture and mattresses go out.
- **Outside the city limits:** Alachua County collects up to 2 cubic yards of bulky items a week from homes with its curbside service. See the county's [curbside pickup](https://alachuacounty.us/Depts/PW/Waste/wasteCollection/Pages/CurbsidePickup.aspx) page.

Getting a couch or a mattress down the stairs and out to the curb is a moving help task. If something has to come apart to fit through the door, say so in your task, so your helper can plan the time and the tools.

## Cleaning for your deposit

Landlords often check the kitchen, the bathrooms and the floors before they return a deposit. A [light cleaning](/gainesville/light-cleaning) helper can do them once the furniture is out: list the rooms and the bathrooms, say whether supplies are there, and ask for exactly what your landlord checks, like the inside of the oven and the fridge. When it's done, take dated photos of each room.

## The yard, if it's yours to leave tidy

The City collects yard waste every week on your scheduled day: paper lawn and leaf bags or reusable containers, never plastic bags, up to 40 pounds each, at the curb by 7 a.m. Loose leaves and pine needles aren't picked up. A [yard cleanup](/gainesville/yard-cleanup) helper can rake, bag and set it out.

## What it costs

Each service's price guide shows the typical range in Gainesville and what you'd pay at each end, fees included: [moving help](/gainesville/moving-help/cost), [light cleaning](/gainesville/light-cleaning/cost), [furniture assembly](/gainesville/furniture-assembly/cost) and [yard cleanup](/gainesville/yard-cleanup/cost).

The City's and the county's rules change from time to time: their pages have the latest.
